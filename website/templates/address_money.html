One template, two layouts. `compact` comes from the view, off the layout registry, and adds a single class -- `.rows.cards` -- exactly as the prototype builds its compact form. Two template files would duplicate every asset row, venue group and chart in order to change a handful of grid rules. What makes this design the design: **one money column**. Asset headers, venue subtotals, position rows and NFT lines all put their figure in a cell `--col` wide at the same x, so a reader compares down the page rather than across it. The reasons are in `frontend/docs/address_page_dom.rst`: 57 themes an SVG `fill` can follow and a canvas cannot, slices that are real buttons sharing one filter state, and ~200 KB saved. Nothing per-reader may be rendered here beyond the layout itself. The cache entry is keyed on `(address, layout)` and shared by every reader on that layout, so the swap entry arrives as a separate non-cached partial, and the pins and saved order live in the reader's own browser. Empty and hidden: the venue cards are assembled in the browser by *moving* each `.pgroup` out of its asset and into the venue that holds it, and moved back when the reader switches away. Moving rather than copying is the whole design -- a copied position would carry a second element with the same `data-pid`, and a pin would then name two rows. It also means open breakdowns, bound handlers and the position pins survive the switch, because they are the same nodes. Rendering the venue grouping server-side as a second list was the alternative and is worse: it doubles a page that already carries 190 positions, and the two copies would drift the first time one of them was edited. No `data-folding`: the load-more rule ranks assets by what they are worth, and a venue is not an asset. The toolbar hides the control entirely while this list is showing. {% endcomment %}
