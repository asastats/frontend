{% comment %} The error pages' entire stylesheet, inlined. These three pages must render when the application is broken, which is the one situation in which the built stylesheet cannot be relied on: a 500 is often exactly the moment `style.tw.css` is missing, stale, or unreachable. So they link nothing and inherit nothing, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight. What was wrong is that they also looked nothing like the site. Fixed light colours on an implicit white background, `display: table` centring, `
` for vertical rhythm and a paragraph pinned to 400px -- a reader who hits one from a dark-themed site gets a flash of white and a page from another decade. The tokens below are copied from the `asastats` and `asastats-dark` themes in `input.css`, which is the only honest way to match a stylesheet you may not load. They are a copy, so they can drift; `core/tests/test_error_pages.py` compares them against the source and fails when they do. Which theme a reader has chosen is not knowable here -- the choice lives in storage this page will not read, and a 500 page has no business running scripts to find out. `prefers-color-scheme` is the closest honest answer and costs nothing. Included rather than triplicated: if `get_template` works at all then so does `{% include %}` of a sibling, and three hand-maintained copies of a palette are three chances to fix one and forget the others. {% endcomment %}
