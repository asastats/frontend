{% load static %} {% load core_extras %} {% comment %} One NFT collection, built as an asset card. Deliberately the same shell as `snippets/money/asset.html`: `.fitem.mcard`, a five-cell `.chead`, `--col` for the figure. A collection *is* a holding, and giving it its own layout would break the one thing this design promises. Two cells differ in what they hold, not in where they are: * the tile is the collection's initials rather than an asset icon, because a collection has no logo of its own -- `.mono-tile.nft` is the prototype's treatment for exactly this; * `.cid-meta` carries the floor/estimate split as a two-part bar, which is the one fact about a collection that a single figure cannot express. The estimate is what the section totals; the floor is what a marketplace will actually pay today, and the gap between them is the whole risk. Contracts kept from design 1, all of them script hooks: `id="f"` + `.fitem` the filter, `checkOpened`, `pins.js` `.nfticon` + `data-path` `address.js` builds the hover preview from it and hides them while filtering `.val` + `data-val` every figure the currency switch recomputes `data-fallback` `site.js` swaps in the placeholder on a 404 The thumbnails move to the top of the *opened* card, where design 1 keeps them in the closed header. The header here is a five-cell grid with a fixed money column, and a collection of sixty thumbnails would either overflow it or squeeze the name to nothing. What replaces them as the closed row's glance value is the tile and the floor bar, which say what the collection is and what it is really worth.
