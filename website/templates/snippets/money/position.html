{% load core_extras %} {% comment %} One position: the atom of the money-column designs. A position is one program under one asset -- a balance, a stake, an LP share. The row is three cells wide and the middle one is *the money column*: every position in every venue under every asset puts its figure at the same x, which is what lets a reader compare down the page instead of across it. That alignment is the design; if a row here stops using `--col`, the design is gone whatever else survives. `.position` wraps the row *and* its breakdown, rather than the two being siblings. Pinning reorders `.position` elements, and a breakdown left behind when its row moves would sit under a different position entirely -- the kind of fault that looks like a rendering glitch and is actually wrong data. The two link idioms are load-bearing and must stay distinguishable *at rest*, because a reader deciding where to click has not pointed at anything yet: `.out` leaves the site (a venue, an explorer). Underline on hover. `.tdist` acts in place -- opens the breakdown below. Dotted at rest, resolving to solid under the pointer. A figure with no breakdown gets neither, and is just a figure. One divergence from the prototype, deliberately: the row's class is `.position`, not the prototype's `.pos`. `static/js/pins.js` and its jest suite already address positions by that name and are covered to 100%; a cosmetic rename would churn a tested engine to no reader's benefit. The prototype is the spec for the *design*, not for identifiers. `data-pid` is the stable position identifier from `api/position_id.py`. It is what a position pin remembers, and it deliberately does *not* hash the amount: the amount moves, and hashing it would restabilise nothing while destabilising every position that never changed. Where a pid names more than one row -- three do in the reference bundle -- `data-amount` is the witness that tells them apart. See `static/js/pins.js`.
